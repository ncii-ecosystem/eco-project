ECOSYSTEM APPLICATION: AIG-NCII is a crisis of non-consent at scale that regulators are scrambling to control. In the U.S., nearly all 50 states have enacted laws regulating AIG-NCII. We found that over 90% of these laws criminalize individuals for creating, soliciting, or distributing AIG-NCII and do very little to regulate the technologies powering abuse.

Criminalizing individuals does not prevent future abuse. In 2025, Grok was generating over 6700 images per hour. In 2026, nearly 90 schools and 600 students around the world have been impacted by AIG-NCII. Deepfake abuse results in a gendered chilling effect where vulnerable communities retreat from online spaces due to fear of harassment. As long as the ecosystem proliferates, the risk of abuse remains, and the silencing effect persists. Regulation that only criminalizes individuals without targeting the technological ecosystem supporting it is simply not enough to prevent abuse at scale.

What technologies are regulated already? We can use our tech ecosystem to build a clearer policy landscape to see gaps and opportunities. To demo, we read all the enacted intimate deepfake laws tracked by Public Citizen and coded them with our ecosystem. Learn more about our methodology here.

Abstract

The last decade has witnessed a rapid advancement of generative AI technology that significantly scaled the accessibility of AI-generated non-consensual intimate images (AIG-NCII), a form of image-based sexual abuse that disproportionately harms and silences women and girls. There is a patchwork of commendable efforts across industry, policy, academia, and civil society to address AIG-NCII. However, these efforts lack a shared, consistent mental model that clearly situates the technologies they target within the context of a large, interconnected, and ever-evolving technological ecosystem. As a result, interventions remain siloed and are difficult to evaluate and compare, leading to a reactive cycle of whack-a-mole. Through a synthesis of over a hundred primary sources from academia, civil society, industry, and policy, we contribute the first comprehensive map of this ecosystem, including 11 categories of technologies that play one of five roles in facilitating AIG-NCII:

  1. 1 Creation Training Datasets , Generative AI Models , and Generative AI Interfaces
  2. 2 Distribution Distribution Channels
  3. 3 Proliferation & Discovery Deepfake Creation Communities , Search Engines , Advertisement Platforms , and App Stores
  4. 4 Infrastructural Support Developer Platforms , Critical Service Providers
  5. 5 Monetization Payment Processors

Our goal is to bridge the gaps and connect the dots to provide a clear, shared terminology and framework for stakeholders to move into the future of AIG-NCII prevention with clarity and foresight.
