We are mapping the tech ecosystem behind deepfake abuse.

Deepfake abuse is not just a single image, model, website, or app. It's an entire ecosystem. To protect women, girls, LGBTQ+ communities, and other vulnerable groups online, we need to see this ecosystem clearly. In Ding et al. (2026) (ACM EAAMO '26), we contribute the first ecosystem of 11 technologies that facilitate the creation, distribution, proliferation and discovery, infrastructural support, and monetization of AI-generated non-consensual intimate images (AIG-NCII) through a synthesis of 100+ sources across academia, industry, journalism, law & policy, trust & safety, and civil society.

A paper is not enough. NCII-ECOSYSTEM.ORG is our effort to translate the research paper into a visual storytelling project and a living evidence database to educate and raise awareness, promote science communication, and support future interventions. This project is created by Michelle L. Ding, Haya Bugshan, and Maja Mishevska.

What brings you here?

I want a guided tour of the ecosystem ↗ Follow an interactive, animated story about the tech ecosystem powering deepfake abuse I want a database tool for research ↗ Search the evidence database of news articles, papers, laws, reports, etc. and export CSV files I want to contribute to the living database ↗ Add a new source to the evidence database and get acknowledged as a contributor I want survivor support ↗ Find a curated list of resources for reporting and taking down images, legal guidance, etc I want to learn more about the research ↗ Read Ding et al. (2026) and learn more about the methodology and contributions

Impact & Press Coverage

Our AIG-NCII tech ecosystem has been cited by academic publications in ICML and the Journal of Online Trust and Safety. Our taxonomy and framework have informed AI policy and governance work on platform take-down policies (Harvard’s BKC ); opportunities and gaps in regulating the U.S. tech landscape (Berkeley’s CLTC ); and deconstructing the deepfake stack in India (Decode at Boom and Tattle Civic Tech ). Our work has been covered by journalists from Decode .
